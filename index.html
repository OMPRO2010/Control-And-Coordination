01 • CHAPTER OVERVIEW

What is Control & Coordination?

Control and coordination allow organisms to respond to changes in their surroundings. Animals mainly use the nervous system and hormones, while plants use hormones and growth movements.

The nervous system receives information from receptors, processes it and sends instructions to muscles or glands.

02 • CORE CONCEPTS

Learn the Fundamentals

👁️

Stimulus

Any change in the environment that produces a response in an organism.

📡

Receptor

Specialized cells that detect changes such as light, heat, pressure and chemicals.

⚡

Response

The reaction of an organism to a particular stimulus.

🧠

Coordination

Working together of different organs to produce a suitable response.

03 • ANIMAL COORDINATION

Nervous System

⚡ How does it work?

The nervous system helps an organism detect changes in its surroundings and respond quickly.

  1. Receptors detect the stimulus.
  2. Sensory neurons carry the message.
  3. The brain or spinal cord processes it.
  4. Motor neurons carry instructions.
  5. Effectors produce the response.

04 • NERVOUS TISSUE

The Neuron

🧬 What is a neuron?

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system. It carries electrical signals from one part of the body to another.

Dendrites

Receive information.

Cell Body

Contains the nucleus.

Axon

Carries the nerve impulse.

Axon Terminal

Passes the signal forward.

05 • QUICK RESPONSE

Reflex Action

⚡ What is a reflex action?

A reflex action is a quick and automatic response to a stimulus. It usually occurs through the spinal cord without requiring conscious thinking first.

Stimulus → Receptor → Spinal Cord → Motor Neuron → Response

06 • CONTROL CENTRE

The Human Brain

🧠

Forebrain

Controls thinking, memory, reasoning and voluntary actions. It also receives and interprets sensory information.

⚙️

Midbrain

Helps control certain reflexes related to visual and auditory stimuli.

🎯

Hindbrain

Includes the cerebellum, pons and medulla. It helps control balance, posture and involuntary activities.

08 • PLANT COORDINATION

How do Plants Respond?

Plants do not have a nervous system like animals. They coordinate their activities mainly through plant hormones and changes in growth.

☀️

Phototropism

Growth of a plant part in response to light. Shoots generally grow towards light.

🌍

Geotropism

Growth response of a plant to gravity. Roots generally grow towards gravity.

💧

Hydrotropism

Growth of roots towards a source of water.

🌱

Chemotropism

Growth response to a chemical stimulus.

09 • PLANT HORMONES

Chemical Messengers

🌱 Auxin

Promotes cell growth and helps in phototropic movement.

📈 Gibberellins

Promote growth of stems and other plant parts.

🍎 Cytokinins

Promote cell division.

🍂 Abscisic Acid

Helps inhibit growth and is associated with stress responses.

10 • HORMONAL COORDINATION

Endocrine System

Hormones are chemical messengers produced by endocrine glands. They travel through the blood and affect specific target organs.

🧠 Thyroxine

Produced by the thyroid gland and helps regulate metabolism.

⚡ Adrenaline

Helps prepare the body for emergency situations.

🩸 Insulin

Helps regulate blood glucose levels.

📊 Growth Hormone

Helps regulate growth and development.
